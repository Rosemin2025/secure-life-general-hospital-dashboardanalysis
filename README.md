# INTRODUCTION
Secure Life General Hospital is a healthcare institution located in Lilongwe, the capital city of Malawi, in the Central Region of the country. Lilongwe is situated in the central part of Malawi and serves as an important administrative, commercial, and healthcare hub. The hospital operates within this geographical setting, providing medical services to individuals, families, and communities within Lilongwe and its surrounding areas.
# PROBLEM STATEMENT
The management of Secure Life General Hospital has observed changes in patient admissions, outstanding medical bills, and variations in insurance coverage across its departments. The hospital wants to use patient admission, billing, and insurance data to understand its operational performance, monitor payments, and improve management decision making. However, the hospital currently lacks a consolidated report that provides a clear picture of its operational and financial performance.
Therefore, this analysis aims to examine the hospital's patient data set to identify trends in patient admissions, departmental utilization, billing patterns, insurance coverage, and payment status. The findings are expected to provide useful insights that can support hospital management in improving operational skillfulness, strengthening financial management, and making informed strategic decisions.
# DATASET OVERVIEW
The data set contains 1,000 patient records and 11 variables covering patient demographics, admission and discharge dates, departments, diagnoses, total bill amounts, insurance coverage percentages, and payment status. The data covers the period from January 2024 to December 2025.
# INSIGHTS
The analysis disclosed that the General Department recorded the highest number of patients, accounting for 25.2% of all admissions. Female patients represented 51.2% of the data set, while male patients accounted for 48.8%. The average patient age was approximately 46 years.
Financial analysis showed that 56.4% of patient accounts were fully paid, while 25.7% remained pending and 17.9% were partially paid. This indicates that a considerable proportion of patient accounts require further payment monitoring.
The average insurance coverage was 74.86%, although 4.4% of patients had no insurance coverage. Orthopedics recorded the highest average bill amount, followed by Cardiology. The average length of hospital stay was approximately 4.81 days.
# RECOMENDATIONS
1.The hospital should strengthen its financial monitoring systems by regularly reviewing pending and partially paid accounts. 
2.Insurance verification procedures should be improved to ensure accurate coverage assessment and reduce billing discrepancies.
3.The management should optimize staffing and resource allocation based on departmental patient demand. 
4.The hospital should also enhance its patient data management systems to facilitate regular reporting and support evidence based decision making.
# CONCLUSION
The analysis of the Secure Life General Hospital patient data set provides a consolidated overview of patient admissions, departmental utilization, billing patterns, insurance coverage, and payment status.
The findings highlight the importance of effective financial management, appropriate resource allocation, and improved insurance administration. By implementing the recommended measures, hospital management can strengthen operational monitoring, improve revenue collection, and support informed decision making.
